敷衍了事

fū yǎn liǎo shì

In English

  • go through the motions
  • perfunctory

Meaning

Only getting through the task, with no real effort or sincerity.Negative · General

Example

他開會時敷衍了事，顯然只想早點下班。

He just went through the motions at the meeting, clearly counting the minutes.

Usage

About insincerity: not putting in real effort, though the result is not necessarily poor. Also used for brushing off questions or complaints.

Similar

  • 🧹馬虎了事mǎ hǔ liǎo shì

    cut corners; slapdash — Careless and sloppy; about lack of care.

    Don't cut corners on this report; the client will check every figure.

  • 🙄敷衍塞責fū yǎn sè zé

    do the bare minimum; shirk responsibility — Doing the bare minimum and dodging blame; about shirking.

    The officials did the bare minimum, and the public's problem dragged on.

Opposite

  • 🏃全力以赴quán lì yǐ fù

    go all out — Giving it everything.

    The whole team went all out to finish before the deadline.

  • 💪不遺餘力bù yí yú lì

    has gone to great pains; spare no efforts — Sparing no effort.

    The volunteers spared no effort in rescuing the stranded hikers.
