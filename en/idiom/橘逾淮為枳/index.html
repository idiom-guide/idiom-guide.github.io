橘逾淮為枳

jú yú huái wéi zhǐ

In English

  • doesn't travel well
  • what works in one place may fail in another

Meaning

A tangerine planted north of the Huai River becomes a bitter orange: the same thing changes when its surroundings change.Neutral · Formal

Example

全盤照搬國外的政策，往往橘逾淮為枳。

Policies copied wholesale from abroad often don't travel well.

Usage

From the Rites of Zhou (Kao Gong Ji); the Yanzi Chunqiu tells a similar story; also 南橘北枳. Now a warning against transplanting systems, policies or management methods wholesale.

Similar

  • 👞削足適履xuē zú shì lǚ

    procrustean bed — Cutting the foot to fit the shoe: forcing an unsuitable method; 橘逾淮為枳 is about change caused by a new environment.

    Changing the facts to fit the theory is like using a Procrustean bed.

  • 🤒水土不服shuǐ tǔ bù fú

    fail to acclimatize; not suit the local conditions — Failing to adapt to a new place.

    The management system failed to adapt once it reached the branch office.

Opposite

  • 🧩因地制宜yīn dì zhì yí

    adapt to local conditions — Adapting to local conditions: the cure for 橘逾淮為枳.

    Policies should be adapted to local conditions.

  • 🥢入境隨俗rù jìng suí sú

    when in Rome, do as the Romans do — When in Rome, do as the Romans do.

    When in Rome, do as the Romans do: take off your shoes at the door.
